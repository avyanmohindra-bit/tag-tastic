Question Tags

Learn the rules, practise the examples, then take the 50-question challenge.

What are Question Tags?

A question tag is a short question added to the end of a statement. We use question tags when we want confirmation or want to check whether something is true.

You are ready, aren't you?
She likes football, doesn't she?

Rule 1: Positive Statement → Negative Tag

When the main statement is positive, the question tag is usually negative.

Positive statement + negative question tag
Avyan plays football well, doesn't he?
She is happy, isn't she?

Rule 2: Negative Statement → Positive Tag

When the statement is negative, the question tag is usually positive.

Negative statement + positive question tag
Darsh isn't late, is he?
They don't play today, do they?

Rule 3: Use the Helping Verb

The helping verb in the statement is normally repeated in the question tag.

She is studying, isn't she?
They have finished, haven't they?
He will come, won't he?

Rule 4: Modal Verbs

With modal verbs such as can, could, should, would, must and will, use the same modal verb in the tag.

Avyan can score, can't he?
You should study, shouldn't you?
They will arrive soon, won't they?

Rule 5: Imperatives

Imperatives are commands or requests. Their question tags often use "will you?"

Open the door, will you?
Pass the ball, will you?
Don't make noise, will you?

Special Case: Let's

When a sentence begins with "Let's", the usual question tag is "shall we?"

Let's go outside, shall we?
Let's practise, shall we?

Special Case: I am

The special question tag for "I am" is "aren't I?"

I am late, aren't I?
I am doing well, aren't I?

Special Words with Negative Meaning

Words such as never, nobody, nothing, hardly, rarely and seldom already give a negative meaning. Therefore, they normally take a positive question tag.

He never complains, does he?
Nobody called, did they?
Nothing happened, did it?

Important Exceptions

Some expressions have special forms. For example:

Let's go, shall we?
Open the door, will you?
I am late, aren't I?
You'd better hurry, hadn't you?
